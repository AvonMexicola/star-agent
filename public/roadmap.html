Flight physics and the edge of space

The ship feels the atmosphere. Two flight regimes with a defined boundary, and landing struts that absorb the shock.

AltitudeRegimeBehaviour
> 70 kmSPACE · 6-DOFNewtonian. RCS translation on all axes, torque control, flight-assist damping toggle, no drag. Speed capped only by travel mode.
20 – 70 kmTRANSITIONAero forces scale with density ρ(h) = ρ₀·e−h/8000. Control authority blends from RCS to control surfaces by dynamic pressure q = ½ρv². Re-entry glow when q·v exceeds threshold. HUD reads "ATMO 43 %".
< 20 kmPLANE FLIGHTLift and drag from angle of attack and airspeed, stall below vstall, banked turns, thrust-limited top speed. Gravity kept at 1 g for feel, exposed as a constant.

Work items

  • flight-model.js, a pure step(state, controls, env, dt), unit-tested for stall, terminal velocity and RCS in vacuum. ASTRA
  • Regime blend and HUD indicator; per-regime and per-mode speed limits. ASTRA
  • landing-gear.js: spring-damper strut per leg (0.6 m travel), one raycast per strut against terrain or hangar deck, touchdown g-force to a damage event. ASTRA · SUBAGENT
  • Re-entry plasma shader on the hull driven by q. ASTRA · SUBAGENT
  • Wind loudness from q, RCS puffs in vacuum. ASTRA · AUDIO

Why 70 km

The Kármán line at quarter scale would be about 25 km, but the scattering shell already rendered ends at 70 km. Using the shell as the boundary keeps what the pilot sees and what the ship feels in agreement.

EXITOrbit to landing with continuous behaviour change; a hard landing bounces on the struts; six-axis strafe works in space and not on the deck.

Cockpit: MFDs, radar sphere, power

Four standardized rectangular displays in the corners of the pilot's view, a radar sphere between the lower two, all data-driven and cheap.

MFD standard

  • 512 × 384 canvas (4:3) drawn to a cockpit quad or a DOM overlay; same page code either way.
  • Pages: CONTACTS (range, closing speed, IFF), POWER (classic pips: weapons / thrusters / shields, afterburner reserve), SHIP (damage per component, fuel, gear, mode), NAV (destination, ETA, regime, hangar guidance), COMMS / CARGO later.
  • Five bezel buttons per side. 1–4 selects an MFD, [ ] pages.
  • Radar sphere: instanced points and rings, up to 256 contacts coloured by IFF, elevation stalks, 20 Hz.

Efficiency contract

  • Each page is a pure draw(ctx, shipState).
  • Displays refresh at 12 Hz, staggered one per frame, and only when their dirty hash changes.
  • One typed-array ShipState bus written by the sim; no DOM reads in the loop.
  • Budget under 1 ms per frame on a laptop, gated in the smoke test.

Combat

Wing Commander-style dogfighting inside 4 km. Travel mode versus combat mode, shields that matter.

Modes

Travel is fast with weapons safe and shields low. Combat caps speed at 250 m/s, afterburner to 400 m/s on a heat and fuel budget, weapons hot, gimbal assist on. Switching takes 1.5 s and the POWER page pips redistribute.

Weapons on mounts

Hardpoint sockets at wing tips, nose and belly. Laser repeaters (energy, travel time), ballistic cannons (projectiles, ammo), missiles (lock-on, countermeasures). Hard 4 km range, lead indicator from target velocity, projectiles in a fixed 60 Hz sub-step.

Shields and damage

Four quadrants with pools, recharge after 3 s without a hit, power pips scale recharge. Hull HP per component: engines, wings and gear each degrade a gameplay system. Impact ripple shader, sparks, smoke on damaged engines.

AI

Three behaviours: pursue with lead, strafe and extend, evade. Difficulty by turn rate and accuracy. Patrol encounters near the station and the ring. Single-player first, server-authoritative once Phase 5 lands.

Multiplayer

A shared solar system with dozens of ships in view and persistent bases. The client stays a static site; the server ships entities, never terrain.

Architecture

  • Authoritative server in TypeScript (Node 22 or Bun) or Rust, fixed 30 Hz tick.
  • WebTransport preferred, WebSocket fallback, binary packets, client prediction with reconciliation.
  • Interest management by spatial cell per body: full updates within about 50 km, low-rate beacons for everything else.
  • One shard process per body (Aeon, Sel, Pyre, ring, deep space). All fit on one machine early; a gateway hands players between shards on jump later.
  • Postgres for accounts, inventories, bases and economy; Redis pub/sub between shards. Magic-link or GitHub sign-in, no stored passwords.
  • Server owns damage, resources and money; the client owns only its own flight input.

Rules of thumb

  • About 25 KB/s down and 5 KB/s up per player at 20 Hz with 50 entities in interest.
  • About 0.3 ms CPU per player-tick at 30 Hz in JavaScript, so roughly 250 players per core.
  • RAM is negligible. Bandwidth is what you pay for.
  • Put a Cloudflare tunnel and DDoS front in place before any public announcement.

Bases, resources, mining, caves

Rust and Dune-style modular building, a harvest-refine-craft chain, and caves where the rare items are.

Building

Socket-snapped pieces: 4 m foundation, wall, doorway, window, roof, ramp, pillar, gate, in wood, stone and metal tiers. Levelling foundations sit on the terrain height function; bases up to 1 000 pieces; placement validated server-side; ownership with an access code.

Interference shield

The base core projects a sphere of 150 – 600 m by tier. Ships without the code get a NO ENTRY warning on the MFD at twice the radius and lose engines inside it. People on foot can still walk in, so walls matter. A faint hex bubble marks the boundary.

Resources and tools

Wood from trees (chopping removes the instance and it regrows), stone from rocks and cliffs, metal ore from veins in cliffs, caves and ring asteroids, crystals only in caves. Hatchet, pick and a mining laser with a heat budget.

Processing chain

Campfire, then forge (ore to ingots), refinery (fuel from ice and regolith), fabricator (parts, weapons, base pieces). Inventory by mass, ship cargo hold, storage crates.

Voxel mining: asteroids and planetside

Every mineable volume is a signed-distance field meshed with marching cubes in a worker, the same engine for ring asteroids, cave interiors and ore outcrops. An asteroid is an ellipsoid plus noise plus an ore-vein field, voxelized in 32³ chunks only within about 2 km of the player. Mining subtracts a sphere; each asteroid keeps a sparse brush list of position, radius and material removed, so holes are tiny to persist, cheap to replicate and identical for every player. On the planet the height-field stays authoritative for the surface and voxel islands overlay it at ore outcrops, cliff faces, cave interiors and under a placed excavation marker. Digging the open surface anywhere is out of scope for v1, since a height-field cannot hold an overhang. Yield equals removed volume times ore density at the brush.

Caves

A 3D worm-noise field carves the terrain. Near the player it is voxelized in 32³ chunks at 1 m and meshed with marching cubes in a worker; entrances appear where the field meets steep terrain. Interiors are lit by crystal emissives and a headlamp. Rare items spawn deep. The height-field terrain stays as it is; cave chunks replace it locally with a stencil on the surface mesh.

Hero props with Meshy (25 000 credits)

A Claude Opus agent drives Meshy through Chrome while you stay signed in, the one standing exception to the token policy because it needs Claude's browser tooling; it never handles credentials. Targets: forge, refinery, fabricator, storage crate, base gate, turret bases, mining laser, shop kiosk, an asteroid set and a crystal set, each under 10 k triangles, decimated in headless Blender and exported as glTF into the models folder. Check the per-model credit cost in the dashboard first; the budget should cover on the order of a hundred assets, so plan around forty and keep the rest for iterations.

Economy and pre-built outposts

Somewhere to sell what you mined and buy what you need.

Outposts

One pre-built NPC outpost per biome plus the station concourse, each with shops for resources, fuel, ammo and ship parts. Prices drift with server-wide supply and demand. A mission board offers deliveries, escorts, patrol clears and cave surveys.

Money

Currency and ledger live on the server. Player-to-player trade happens at kiosks. Cave crystals and artefacts are the high-value goods, which is what makes the caves worth the risk.

EXITA full loop in under thirty minutes of play: mine, sell, buy a weapon, win a fight, repair.

Community model, decided 2026-09-05

The repository is private and invitation-based. Anyone who wants to contribute by bringing their own coding agent, whether Claude Opus 5, Fable, GPT-6 or Sol, asks Cees for an invite. The agents guide and the handoff document are the onboarding: file ownership, the module-swap pattern, test pages under /dev/, and tests that stay green. Invitees work on branches and open pull requests, and the agents' owners review each other's work.

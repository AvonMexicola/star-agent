## Ranked closures

1. **Give all three fixed boarding treads an actual load path.** Images 01, 02 and 04 show the two lower plates floating. `build_mining_rover.py` creates only tread boxes and grip strips at lines 137–140. The upper tread intersects the floor region, but that does not support the lower two. Add two visible stringers under the tread edges, joined into the cabin sill/keel, within the existing port envelope. Tread centres are X −1.55/−1.19/−0.94 and Y 0.16/0.34/0.44; their thickness is 0.045 m. Keep the walking tops and outboard door sweep clear. Recheck added supports against wheels and the full entry path. The prior CPU wording about a floor support joint described the upper tread only; it did not certify a complete stair structure.
2. **Remove the repeating surface artefacts and tyre edge colour.** Images 03/04 show diagonal stripes on surfaces that should have distinct ceramic, polymer and metal finishes; 01/04/06 show orange lines around the tyres. The texture builder's periodic scuff formula and per-face atlas projection are relevant sources to inspect. Keep rubber consistently dark and use material-specific roughness and localized wear. Do not simply increase normal intensity or add more scratches. This is a visible defect; the exact origin of every orange pixel has not been independently isolated.
3. **Correct the front identity orientation.** `BURROW M-04` is upside down in 01 and especially 04. The front text's current Blender X −90° rotation makes its face point forward while its local up points down. A front-facing transform with local text up along game +Y is required. The rear `MERIDIAN` is upright in 06; preserve that orientation. The roof mark has a readable orientation from the rear and is not the same defect.
4. **Develop the cab, fenders and storage into a credible manufactured body.** Preserve the measured cabin, wheel and cargo envelopes. Add deliberate cap/return profiles to the roof and fenders, supported transitions around the cab corners and meaningful lid/service seams on the storage. The broad front panel and thin horizontal shelves currently overpower the smaller hardware. This calls for local construction refinement, not making the rover larger or cutting into the occupied cabin.
5. **Resolve small attachment/readability details once the broad surfaces are clean.** The work lamps need a legible mount to the frame; the paired tool sleeves need a more controlled cylindrical highlight/edge finish at the pilot's normal viewing distance. Keep their small root radii and measured articulation clearances. Judge the cockpit controls with the actual live display and driver interaction in the separate game pass rather than adding labels to compensate for a blank fixture screen.

The parent has proposed a label correction, under-tread stringers, material changes and local roof/cassette/fender profiles. Those proposals address this review, but no unrendered change is awarded a higher score. A changed export needs a new identity and targeted visual/clearance follow-up.

## Fixture limitations and correction

The pictures show a properly standing figure and an actually seated figure in the cabin. However, I found an error in **my fixture's pre-render numerical human bounds**: `sync()` calls `updateWorldMatrix()`, which bypasses `SkinnedMesh.updateMatrixWorld()`'s attached-skin `bindMatrixInverse` refresh. `capture.json` therefore includes doubled/stale translations in `placedPosedBounds` and in the fit boxes for 01/02. For example, the standing root is at X −2.45 while its recorded bounds are incorrectly near X −4.9; the seated record incorrectly reports a top at Y 2.563. Those numerical bounds must not be cited as a clearance certificate.

The renderer subsequently calls `scene.updateMatrixWorld()` before drawing, which refreshes the skin inverse. The viewed figures are visibly in their intended places; the mistake leaves unnecessary framing space rather than proving a changed rover or a floating rendered human. The correct independent CPU skinning audit uses joint-world × inverse-bind matrices and remains valid: the seated human bounds are approximately X ±0.42943, Y [0.57845, 1.95963], Z [−1.33649, −0.39939]. Its separate candidate 04 head-rest/backpack result is not derived from this browser metadata.

Future fixture measurements must first call `scene.updateMatrixWorld(true)`, then update the skeleton, and assert placed bounds against those CPU coordinates. Preserve this capture and its metadata rather than silently replacing the failed proof. The phone overview shows the full open-door asset but uses only about 24% of image height; it proves neither touch controls nor fine detail at a practical phone inspection zoom. The underbody has its floor hidden and evaluates full droop; its negative wheel Y is a posed diagnostic, not an observed terrain penetration. The aimed/compressed detail frame also keeps the body fixed, so it does not simulate settled tyre contact.

Recorded scene costs are 36 calls / 24,062 triangles for each human exterior, 18 / 13,682 for the cockpit, 32 / 19,750 for cutter detail, 31 / 19,792 for the underside, and 33 / 19,794 for rear/phone. These include fixture objects and the human where visible, and vary with view culling. They are not asset-only costs, texture-residency measurements or FPS results.

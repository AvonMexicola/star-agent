# Atlas silhouette review 01

**Silhouette and scale: 4.0/5. The required 4.5 silhouette gate is not met.**
The ship now reads clearly as a large drive-through hauler. The swept shoulders,
lower middle roof, short aft hump and paired recessed engines improve its
outline. The tall dorsal side still reads as a long box, however, and the
deployed gear doors add three conspicuous shelves per side. More primary-form
work is needed before the material pass. This is not a final six-criterion or
merge approval.

## What now works

The 1.8 m mannequin beside the bow and landing pad makes the scale convincing:
the foot is wider than a person is tall, the loading mouth accommodates large
cargo and the cockpit sits a full deck above it. The retained hold's long lane,
bay racks, gates and call panel provide consistent human-scale cues. The heavy
hauler role is substantially clearer than the original 3.0 baseline.

The planform now broadens toward the rear shoulders instead of remaining
parallel. The bridge wedge and twin power pods remain distinct at distance.
The smaller engine topology retains real recessed outlets; the direct stern
view shows the throat and centre plugs. I am not judging final engine material
finish or emission in this gate.

[geometry-audit.json](geometry-audit.json), generated by the independent
[CPU script](geometry-audit.mjs), confirms **59,443 triangles / 3,859,404 bytes**,
241 mesh primitives, 13 materials and 10 images. Four runtime MFD quads bring
the visible ship count to **59,451**, leaving only **549 triangles** below the
60k ceiling. Each drive is now 3,796 triangles and the interior is 25,020.
Actual decoded bounds are approximately **61.789 m long × 35.535 m wide ×
14.943 m high**. The deployed minimum y is −8.75e−8 m, numerical noise rather
than the previous substantial toe penetration. This report does not independently
repeat the root's dense ramp or hold-clearance checks.

## Ranked closures

1. **Change the dorsal side section, not just its roof line.** In
   [side](desktop-side-stowed.png) and [quarter](desktop-exterior-stowed.png),
   the wall between y 9.35 and roughly 13.1 remains a long, nearly uninterrupted
   slab. The narrow braces decorate that slab without changing its main volume.
   In `fleet_hull.py::build_dorsal_shell`, retain the successful low roof and
   aft hump but give the side a continuous intermediate chine. A useful trial
   is a lower root near |x| 8.3 at y 9.35, an outward chine around |x| 9.1 at
   y 11.1, then the retained shoulder near |x| 7.8 at y 13.1 (13.42 forward).
   This makes two deliberate angled bands around the protected pressure walls.
   Preserve the exact room/ceiling limits in the
   [constraints audit](../production-constraints/review.md); do not lower the
   interior ceiling or shrink the cargo lane to achieve the shape.

2. **Clear and properly seat both forward S3 interfaces.** My exact triangle
   clipping finds foreign geometry in the standard radius .875 m cylinder,
   extending from .25 m below to 1.2 m above each mating plane. Port has five
   intersecting triangles and starboard four: shoulder armour plus the forward
   raked buttress. The nearest brace radii are **.820143/.820257 m**, about
   **55 mm inside** the reserved interface. One port witness is
   **(−10.754185, 10.100000, −8.311608)**; the starboard result is symmetric.
   Move the complete forward buttress at least about .5 m aft as a first trial,
   then recheck its full thickness and ceramic return. Recess the shoulder
   around (±11.2, −9) in XZ to at most y 8.62 within a roughly 1 m radius,
   keeping the mating origin at y 8.9. Fit a continuous collar into that recess.
   The current flat load-pad stock visibly emerges as a triangular wedge
   through the sloping ceramic beside the socket. The CPU JSON records exact
   mesh names, triangle indices and contact witnesses for the fix.

3. **Make the deployed doors follow the gear-bay form.** The six real pockets
   and counter-levelled pads work visually, but the long ivory outer leaves
   at 150° read as awnings in [side](desktop-side.png) and
   [gear detail](desktop-gear-down.png). Fold those leaves closer to the bay
   sides, or use a narrow folding leaf if the bow/drive skin prevents further
   rotation. Keep the existing doors-first/leg-second sequence. Check every
   changed sweep against the actual cheek, engine and pad geometry; a different
   angle alone has not been certified here. Reallocate existing topology rather
   than spending the small remaining triangle allowance on decorative hardware.

4. **Finish the loading mouth as one protective shape.** The
   [bow](desktop-bow.png) retains a projecting horizontal crown above two
   separate cheek prongs. In `drive_pods.py::build_loading_bow`, bevel the crown's
   leading face continuously into the upper-bevel endpoints and the cheek
   ridges, instead of leaving a thin flat armour lip over a rectangular beam.
   Preserve the existing opening and measured ramp sweep. The central opening
   is the role-defining feature and should remain fully legible.

5. **Replace incidental triangulation with intentional panel planes.** The
   [plan](desktop-plan.png) and [S3 detail](desktop-s3-forward-support.png) show
   large diagonal plate cuts and triangular tonal breaks across the upper body.
   `_plate()` now avoids some intersecting warped covers by splitting them,
   but exposes each triangulation diagonal as a full armour seam. Use compatible
   section slopes or a small number of explicitly planar strips so the main
   ceramic fields read as manufactured panels. Keep seams at meaningful load
   transitions, not every triangle edge. This is a geometric construction
   closure; a later texture pass should not be used to hide it.

The S3 origins, local +Y normals and dorsal −Z/aft +Z bores match the layout.
The **aft S3 cylinder is clear** of foreign triangles; its pedestal contacts
the flange base in all five support rays. Both forward foundations also meet
their supporting skin in those samples, so I am not calling the sockets
floating. The problem is the intersecting shoulder/pad construction and the
reserved cylinder intrusion. This limited interface check excludes the socket's
own geometry and does not certify any provisional weapon package or installed
weapon.

**Checkpoint recommendation: continue silhouette work; do not advance this
candidate as a passed 4.5 silhouette or final art gate.** The compact topology,
human scale and connected gear are useful progress. The next review should use
a new frozen asset identity and fresh actual-renderer views of the changed
primary forms and the two forward S3 interfaces.
